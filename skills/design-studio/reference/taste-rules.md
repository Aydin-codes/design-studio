# Taste rules: ranked, capped, confirmed

The taste profile has two parts: **Rules** (at most 15, ranked, confirmed by the user) and **Notes** (everything else, unconfirmed). Only Rules steer the work. Notes are evidence.

## Recording a reaction
Every like or dislike the user gives (in chat, on the review board, in a review session) becomes a note with:
- **strength:** love · like · dislike · hate (from their words or their Pick / Reject mark)
- **scope:** always · this kind of project (name it: "finance dashboards", "landing pages") · just this one
- **what and why,** in their words, plus the date and project

Don't guess the scope. If it isn't clear from their words, record "this kind of project" and let repetition sort it out.

Exception: when the user says outright "never" or "always", it's a rule at once (rule 7). Add it at the top and tell them in one line.

## Turning notes into rules
A pattern becomes a **suggested rule** when the same thing appears in notes about 3 or more different sites or projects, with the same direction (all dislikes, or all likes).

Ask about suggested rules **only at the end** of a review session or a run, as one checklist: at most 3 per session, the strongest first. "I noticed: (1) you rejected gray-on-gray cards on 5 sites. Make it a rule? Always / only for apps / no." The rest wait for next time. Never interrupt design work to ask. If the user says "stop asking", keep collecting notes silently.

## Ranking and the cap
- Rules are ranked: rules the user stated outright first, then by how many notes support them, then by how recent.
- At most 15 rules. When a new rule is confirmed and there are already 15, show the lowest-ranked one and ask which to keep. The loser moves to Notes.
- If a new reaction contradicts a rule, ask once at the end which is true now. Don't decide yourself.
- Each rule keeps its scope. A "finance dashboards" rule doesn't apply to a portfolio.

## Older profiles
If the profile has "Loves", "Hates" or "Personal tells" sections instead of Rules and Notes, keep them, treat them as notes, and at the end of the first run offer the user up to 3 of the strongest as rules.

## Format
```
## Rules (max 15, ranked)
1. Never gray cards on a gray page. scope: always. why: "looks faded and AI-made". evidence: 5 sites. confirmed 2026-11-11
2. Dense tables are good. scope: finance dashboards. why: "I want the numbers". evidence: 3. confirmed 2026-11-12

## Notes
- 2026-11-11 dislike, just this one: linear.app pricing: "too much gradient"
```
