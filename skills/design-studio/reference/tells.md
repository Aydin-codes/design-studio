# Common tells (starting set)

Each user's own tells come from their taste profile and take priority. A tell is a look **used by default, with no reason from the subject.** The same look chosen for a reason (a serif because the subject is a printed menu) is fine.

- A slight tilt (rotating a card or image by a degree or two) used as decoration: fake personality on a template
- Second-tier default fonts, the ones Claude picks when told "not Inter": Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, DM Sans, Manrope, Bricolage Grotesque, Space Grotesk, Sora, Urbanist
- Pastel chips or tags, each a different soft color, where the colors mean nothing
- Sections built only from code shapes (boxes, borders, pills, lines, dashed timelines) with no real image, illustration or asset
- A dark "terminal" card with a code font, tree lines and green tags, used to look technical
- Explaining the process (steps, pipelines, diagrams) where the page should show the result
- Claude's decoration grammar: a short colored bar under headings, uppercase mono labels, colored uppercase table headers, one line of colored text as a callout, a row of color swatches, white rounded cards on a gray page
- Labels on the design naming its own fonts, palette or process
- The kit: a small uppercase label (often in a pill), a serif headline, gray body text, a fat pill button, a gray stats line with dots ("4 min · 6 clicks · 4 mistakes"), numbered steps in the accent color
- Several mid-brightness pastels with no leading color; gray cards on a gray panel on a gray page
- Leftover placeholder labels ("group colour 2") or notes explaining the design on the page
- Hand-coded drawings or CSS shapes where a real photo, render or model belongs
- Hero formula: small label, headline with one colored or italic word, gray subtext, a solid and an outline button side by side, a picture on the right
- Fake structure: "02 / Mission" numbering or labels that mean nothing
- Decorative mixing of a thin sans with an italic serif
- Everything centered with ornamental dividers
- No reading order; everything the same weight
- Emoji or generic filler icons
- Gradient circles with initials as avatars
- Glass cards with invented stats; the same number row repeated across screens
- Cards or buttons that do nothing
- Gradient blobs and washes as decoration
- A landing page with no images, or icon grids where product screenshots belong
- A site that says who it's for but looks like it has never met them
- A technique borrowed from a different kind of product (multiplayer cursors with no collaboration)
- Copy that says a lot of nothing
- A marketing page where nothing moves
- Motion as decoration: fade-up on every section, floating blobs, effects that show nothing
